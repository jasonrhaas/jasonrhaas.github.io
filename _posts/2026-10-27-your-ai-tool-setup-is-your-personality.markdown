AI agents are now integrated into almost every tool we use on a daily basis.
At least in the tech industry and the software world, it's very nearly
end-to-end at this point. Which raises the obvious question: *what is the role
of the engineer now?* Or more broadly, *what is the role of the human?*

## Guardrails are good

I see a lot of effort going into putting frameworks, harnesses, and gates
around AI to make it more deterministic. I understand why, and I think the
intentions are good.

I also still think a solid testing framework is essential for software, along
with solid continuous integration and continuous deployment — maybe more
important than ever. Especially end-to-end testing. End-to-end tests,
visibility, and logging have always mattered, but now that we're farther away
from the low-level code than we've ever been, we have to rely on those
indicators even more.

## One size fits nobody

The problem I'm seeing is that people are trying to build frameworks and
workflows meant to be one-size-fits-all — for a whole team, or a whole
engineering org.

By doing that, you take away some of the human input and the human creativity.
AI can already automate a lot of things. If you just lightly ask it, it'll go
reverse-engineer things for you. Standardizing everyone onto the same
prompts and the same flow doesn't add much on top of that. It sands off the
part that was actually yours.

Because here's the point: **your AI configuration is you.** That's your
personality. That's what you're bringing to the table.

## What that looks like for me

I'm pretty heavily integrated with [OpenCode](https://opencode.ai/). I have it
handle Jira for me, write PR descriptions, comment on PRs, create Confluence
documents, think problems through with me — everything.

But it does all of that *in my style*, which I've specified. When it comments
on something in GitHub, I have it add a little caveat:

> Written by OpenCode on behalf of Jason Haas

Because it isn't written by me — but I directed it.

Even the way it codes, the way it writes comments, all of that is my
customized style. I told it to work that way. I've shaped how I want to work
and how I want to communicate, and the agent carries that out.

## That's the engineering

That customization isn't busywork you do before the real work starts. That *is*
the engineering. That's the creative act.

Setting up your agentic workflows to produce optimal results is software
engineering in 2026.
